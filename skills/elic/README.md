# ELIC — Explain Like I’m Curious

ELIC is an AI skill that makes complex concepts understandable across science, mathematics, history, business, technology, arts, and everyday topics. Its goal is to help learners build accurate mental models, explain ideas in their own words, and reason independently—without assuming a particular background.

## Explanation routing is the heart

Different questions need different teaching approaches. ELIC considers what the learner is asking, what they have demonstrated they understand, and where the difficulty lies. It then chooses one primary technique, with up to two supporting techniques unless the topic needs more.

| Learning need | Explanation route |
| --- | --- |
| Missing foundations | First principles |
| Origins or change over time | Historical / evolutionary |
| Alternatives and trade-offs | Comparative |
| How something works | Mechanistic |
| Abstract or invisible ideas | Analogical |
| Mistakes and breakdowns | Failure-driven |
| Connected causes and feedback | Systems thinking |
| Learning by trying | Experimental |
| Misconceptions | Guided discovery |
| Numerical relationships | Worked example |

For example, “How can a profitable business run out of cash?” can use a failure-driven explanation supported by a small numerical example: a shop records a profit but must pay its bills before customers pay it. A follow-up about calculating cash flow can shift the route toward a worked calculation.

The learner asks naturally; ELIC selects the approach and adjusts it as the conversation develops.

## Depth and adaptation

ELIC starts with a **working understanding**: the meaning, broad mechanism, concrete example, and an important limitation. It can move toward a quick intuition or deeper exploration of evidence, formal models, and open questions. Requested length and format take priority.

Unfamiliar terms are defined when needed. Examples fit the learner and subject, analogies include their limits, and uncertainty stays visible. Follow-ups deepen the relevant part, change the explanation, or address confusion without repeating the whole lesson. Understanding checks are occasional and optional.

Success means the learner can connect the parts, apply the idea to a new situation, and recognize its limits.
